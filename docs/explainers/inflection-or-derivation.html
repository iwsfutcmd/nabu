Nabu · lexeme model · decided: two kinds of head (ADR 0145)

Inflection or derivation? Two kinds of head, or one

We decided that every complex word is built the same way: Merge a base lexeme with another lexeme. ran is RUN + PAST; writer is WRITE + AGENT, the lexeme whose lemma is ‑er; شايف is the verb + the participle pattern. The question this page was written for was whether those second lexemes, the heads, come in two kinds. They do: you chose option A of section 7, and ADR 0145 records it. The page keeps the argument: what the kinds are, what a derivative means for a learner, what in Nabu depends on the kinds, the cases that sit on the line, and the three ways it could have been settled.

value head a value of a dimension the base already has; its result is a form of the base's lexeme function head takes a category and makes one; its result is a derivative: another word, computed listed stored, because no rule gives it; a listed derivative is a lexeme

Revised after your questions. A function head is a lexeme too (AGENT, lemma ‑er). A computed derivative is not a learning item, so "lexeme" now means what is stored. What was called a “cell” is now a form. New: the side-by-side table in section 3, and section 4 on what a derivative means for a learner.

Brought up to the accepted model on 2026-11-20 (ADRs 0145 to 0151). Two kinds of head, by dimension, is decided. A lexeme's part of speech is a UD tag, one per lexeme. apc's participle pattern carries two heads. Progress counts a lexeme's listed parts, not its forms. Sections 1, 3, 4, 5.1, 5.4, 5.6, 6 and 7 changed.

1. What was decided first, and what this page settled

Decided: one operation. A base lexeme merges with another lexeme, and the selecting one, the head, supplies the result's features; whatever the head leaves unspecified comes up from the base. The feature is what merges, not its suffix: RUN + PAST is realised as ran because RUN lists that form, and as ‑ed elsewhere.

Decided since (round 4, option A of section 7): heads come in two kinds, which change what the result is:

RUNverb lexeme+ PASTlemma ‑ed · value of Tense→ ran · a form of RUN. Knowing run without ran is not knowing RUN.
WRITEverb lexeme+ AGENTlemma ‑er · function V → N→ writer "one who writes" · a derivative: another word, computed. Knowing write without writer is still knowing WRITE.
كَتَبَverb lexeme+ ACTIVE PARTICIPLElemma فاعِل · function, from a verb→ كاتِب "one who writes" · a derivative: it declines for case and definiteness, which no verb does.

The rows differ only in the head's kind. That one property is what this page is about.

3. In Nabu, the kind belongs to the head

The model already has the pieces. A value head is a value of a dimension that the base's category has: PAST of the verb's tense, PL of the noun's number, 1SG of the verb's person. Its result stays inside the base's lexeme, as a form, and is listed only where no rule gives it (ran, fue, كُتُب).

A function head takes a category and makes one, with a meaning of its own. It is a lexeme just as a value head is, named here by its meaning, with its affix or pattern as its lemma: AGENT (‑er), ACTIVE PARTICIPLE (فاعِل), PLACE (مَفْعَل), DIMINUTIVE (‑ito), Uzbek AGENT (‑chi). Productivity doesn't make it a lexeme; it decides whether its results are computed (writer "one who writes") or listed (warmth, from unproductive ‑th). Its result is a derivative: lexeme-shaped (a category, a paradigm, a gloss) but computed from its parts, a value like scanned, not a stored lexeme. Where something about it is listed, it is stored, and it is a lexeme: كاتِب "clerk", with its plural كُتّاب.

The test that decides a head's kind: is it one of a set of alternatives that every member of the base's category must choose from? Every Spanish verb form has a person and a tense, so person and tense are dimensions and their values are value heads. No noun must choose between plain and diminutive, so the diminutive is a function head. The dimensions are declared once per language, one list per category: the paradigm. A category is written as its UD tag, one per lexeme. A regular base-case morpheme checks a dimension and alone doesn't make one: Spanish ‑ito and the Uzbek causative have one, and nothing makes a word choose them.

Value heads don't have to sit inside one word. A form can be several words, which grammars call periphrasis (Ackerman & Stump 2004): Spanish he comido, Persian خواهم رفت "I will go", Cantonese 食咗 sik6 zo2 "ate". Since Merge goes above the word, a form realised by two tokens costs nothing extra.

A form is the word itself, inflected; a derivative is another word made from it.

4. What a derivative means for a learner

A regular derivative can be built entirely from its parts, so, like scanned for ‑ed, it has nothing of its own to learn. That's why "lexeme" is kept for what is stored: a stored lexeme is learned, and a computed derivative is a value.

  • Progress lives on its parts. writer "one who writes" in a sentence is evidence for WRITE, for AGENT and for the branch of the rule it used. Credit per node makes it exact: ✗writing for writer shows WRITE known and AGENT missed. A derivative never gets a word-list entry or a count, so nothing ephemeral needs tracking.
  • Which ones a learner meets: whatever the course's sentences and stories contain, as scanned turns up for ‑ed. The planner aims at the head and its branches: the Arabic participle shown on a sound root (فاعِل), a hollow one (قائِل) and a defective one (رامٍ), each on a word the learner has never seen in that form. The compiler can report a branch that nothing in the course shows.
  • Infinitely many is the point. Knowing AGENT is being able to make any of them; the wug card, last and always labelled as made up, checks exactly that.
  • Once anything about it is listed, it's a lexeme, stored and learned: writer "author", كاتِب "clerk", panadero (‑ero means a trade, a container or a tree, and nothing predicts which), ahorita ("right now" or "in a while" by region, neither of them "now" plus a diminutive). The computed ones are the transparent, productive heads: Arabic participles and the verbal nouns of Forms II–X, Spanish ‑mente, English ‑ness.

So a regular form and a regular derivative are alike for a learner: both are evidence for their parts. What differs is the head (a choice made every time, or a meaning added when wanted) and where a listed one lives. The apc participle looked hard because its pattern carries two heads (decided in round 5; Kline 2022, ch. 17). The participle is a value of aspect: it contrasts with رحت and بروح, like a form. The agentive noun is a function head on the same pattern: a derivative, which takes the article, and a lexeme where its plural or its vowels are listed (معلّم m3allem "teacher"). A woman writing is ketbe; a woman who is a writer, ketibe.

5. What depends on the kind

Six things in Nabu read it. For each, a mockup with the distinction kept (A) beside everything treated as one kind, all function heads (B). A is what was decided.

5.1 Progress and "learned"

A lexeme is learned when every part the course will teach has been shown, and its listed forms are among those parts. What is counted is listed parts, not forms: a regular verb has one part, itself (hablar · regular · 1 of 1), and a form the rules give is evidence for the rules. A derivative isn't part of its base: a computed one counts towards nothing (its parts take the credit), and a listed one is a lexeme of its own.

5.2 Where a listed form lives

The learner's test decides it: if knowing X without Y is not fully knowing X, Y is part of X. ran is part of RUN; writer "author" isn't part of WRITE. Arabic shows both in one family: كُتُب "books" is a listed form of كِتاب, while كاتِب "clerk" is a lexeme of its own, with its own listed plural كُتّاب; the participle "one who writes" takes the plural a rule gives, كاتِبُون. Without kinds, every listed form is a lexeme, and the test has nothing to sort.

5.3 Glosses

Decided in round 3: a form's gloss is built from its lexeme's plus its features, shown beside the support language's own word (“ran” beside “run (past)”). A derivative gets the gloss its parts give, or its own where it's listed; a literal hint stays separate.

Under B both panels take the second shape: fue becomes a derivative of ser, glossed "ser + preterite 3sg" like a new word, and the panel can no longer say it is a form of ser.

5.4 Which cards can be built

ADR 0132 already splits gap-fill cards by what they ask: which word (other lexemes with the answer's part of speech and features) or which form (the answer's own lexeme in other attested forms). "Which form" asks for another form of the same lexeme, so the kind decides what can be an option. (Since ADR 0147 a story line may ask a third thing, which use, for a contrast only the passage decides.) Here is the apc participle, which section 6 comes back to:

The pattern's other head, the agentive noun (معلّم "teacher"), is a word of its own, so it is asked as "which word".

5.5 Grading: grammatical or lexical miss

The miss labels (decided) split "right lexeme, wrong form" from "another lexeme". That split is the kind.

Under B, hablas would be "another word, from the same base" too: the feedback would tell a learner who knows hablar perfectly well that they chose the wrong word.

5.6 Order and prompts

Two smaller things. Order: linearisation can lean on the kinds (function heads attach first, value heads outside them, as in ishchilarimiz) instead of a slot per affix. Prompts: the tags that pin down what the prompt leaves open and the answer must say, "(a woman speaking)", "(to several people)", "(every day)", "in Cuba", come from a dimension's value or from a scope (a variety, a register: ADR 0147). A function head never raises one: nobody tags a prompt "(diminutive)".

7. Three ways it could be settled, and the one chosen

OptionWhat it saysWhat it costsWhat breaks
A. Two kinds, by dimensionEach language declares its categories' dimensions. A head that is a value of one is a value head; every other head is a function head. Nothing is labelled affix by affix.Writing the dimensions down per language (most are in the courses' UD features already).Nothing in section 5. Hard cases (Russian aspect, the apc participle) become questions about a language's dimensions, which is where they belong.
B. One kindEvery head is a function; every result is a derivative.Rebuilding progress, glosses, form cards and grammatical misses on "same base, sibling heads".Those four need "heads that are alternatives in one dimension" to work at all, which is option A under another name. Without it: 5.1 to 5.5 as shown.
C. Two kinds, labelledEach head says which it is.A label on every affix and pattern.Labels drift from what the paradigm actually is; two courses of one language can disagree.

Decided: A (round 4; ADR 0145). Kinds follow from dimensions, and dimensions are data per language. It keeps your "one operation" (a value head and a function head merge the same way), gives every consumer in section 5 what it needs, and moves the hard cases to the right question: does this language's verb have an aspect dimension with a participle in it?

The three follow-ups, as decided

  1. Where dimensions live: per language, beside the lexicon, one list per category, a UD tag (apc verb: aspect, person, gender, number; Spanish noun: number; Spanish adjective: gender, number). Gender on nouns stays a lexical feature, not a dimension, stored only where no default gives it: casa's comes from its ‑a, and mano stores its own.
  2. apc's participle: a value of an aspect dimension (perfective, imperfective, participle), so the form card in 5.4 can be built. Decided since (round 5): the pattern carries a second head as well, the agentive noun, a function head (section 6).
  3. Periphrastic forms: a form may be several tokens (he comido, خواهم رفت, 食咗), since Merge goes above the word.
